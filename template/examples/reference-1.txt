Economy and markets.

Fed. Mechanism: Waller argues for a larger, fifty basis point December hike on top of a range already at three point seven five to four percent. Intended effect: re-anchor inflation expectations; a Taylor rule with inflation above target and real rates near zero says policy is still too loose. Risks: the supply-shock school says hiking into an energy shock cuts demand without lowering oil, while the Phillips-curve school fears expectations drifting if the Fed waits. Read-through: a bigger hike lifts the dollar, pressures long-duration tech and AI financing, and tightens leverage costs. Reports describe a December hike as close to locked in; size is the open question.

UK. Inflation at three point six percent is cost-push, not demand-pull. With unemployment steady at four point six percent, no slack is opening to absorb it, so the Phillips curve favours the Bank of England's hawks. For sterling, the relative pace of Bank and Fed hikes is the variable.

Anthropic. Priced at eighty-three dollars, raising eighty-one point three billion dollars, the biggest IPO ever, trading on Nasdaq today. Absorbing that supply is itself a test of appetite for other AI names; that is my inference.

Elsewhere. Australia's unemployment jumped to four point nine percent, a five-year high, after yesterday's wage strength: a stagflation-lite mix. The US could hit its debt limit by February, says the Bipartisan Policy Center, and the IMF warns global public debt is heading for a hundred percent of GDP, the highest since the Second World War.

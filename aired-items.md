# Recently aired consumer items

The second memory file, alongside `story-threads.md`. Read it at the start of
every draft and check every gadget, camera, projector and car item against it
before using one.

## Why this file exists

`story-threads.md` tracks news threads. Nothing tracked consumer items, and the
drafting agent starts cold every morning, so a product that ran on Monday looks
brand new again on Wednesday. On 9 September a projector that had aired on the
7th was drafted a second time, in *less* detail than the first outing, and was
caught only because the session compared it against the committed briefs by
hand. The spec is explicit that the owner cannot catch this himself:

> "I have no memory between runs and cannot catch repetition myself."

From 10 September the list below was passed to the drafting agent by hand every
morning. It earned its place: it has caught a re-run on most days since,
including three separate iPhone Duo items on the 11th, a verbatim repeat of the
photomask standard the same day, and the XWALL projector on at least seven
separate days.

## The rule

A **restatement** is not news. A **genuine follow-on** is: a missing price now
published, a ship date confirmed, a reversal, a rumour becoming a real product,
a launch actually happening. Say it as a follow-up when it is one.

The OM System PEN is the worked example of the distinction. It ran on
7 September as a rumour, on the 9th as a real product with pre-orders open, and
on the 12th with a late-October release date and a pre-order backlog — three
airings, all legitimate, each carrying a fact the last one did not have. It was
then correctly declined on the 10th, 16th, 20th and 21st when what surfaced was
a designer interview, a reviews round-up and a design-history piece, none of
which carried a price or a final sensor spec.

## Pruning

Drop entries once they are about a fortnight old and have stopped resurfacing.
Keep anything that keeps coming back regardless of age — the XWALL projector has
reappeared in the digest on seven separate days since it aired and would have
been re-run at least twice without this list.

## Recently used weather angles

Weather is the one section written for pleasure, and its jokes wear out faster
than anything else in the brief. A session that starts cold each morning cannot
feel that, so record the angle used each day here and read it before writing.

**The two constructions that wore out, and the rule:**
- *The walk-back* — "I told you X, it's Y." Ran 9, 10, 11 and 12 September, four
  days straight, and went stale. Rested from the 13th and every opener since has
  been better for it. It is genuinely funny about **once a fortnight**, when the
  forecast has actually made a fool of the brief. Not otherwise.
- *The self-contradicting label* — making "clearsky, 45.8mm rain" itself the
  joke. Used 8 September ("somebody has stapled two days together and gone to
  lunch") and 16 September ("the adjective and the number are written by two
  processes that have never spoken"). Twice in eight days was already too often.
  The data throws these up constantly; the joke should not follow every time.

**Angles used, most recent first.** Do not repeat one inside a week.
- 21 Sep — the twelve-degree daily swing; dressing for two climates in one day.
- 20 Sep — satisfaction that the promised dry spell arrived.
- 19 Sep — today is the wet one, straightforwardly.
- 18 Sep — one clipped correction clause, then straight to the forecast.
- 17 Sep — owning a real miss: "the forecast did not escalate, it evaporated."
- 16 Sep — the self-contradicting label (see above).
- 13 Sep — the warning was right for once, and worse than advertised.
- 8 Sep — the self-contradicting label, first use.

**Two standing rules, whatever the angle.** Lead on the rain figure rather than
the label when they disagree - the number is what someone dresses for. And name
a day that breaks the pattern ONLY when one genuinely does; several weeks have
had none, and inventing one is worse than saying the week is uniform.

Economy and markets.

Tariffs. Mechanism: the US trade representative proposed Section three-oh-one tariffs from early next year: fifteen percent on the EU, Taiwan and South Korea, eighteen on most others, up to twenty-five on India and thirty-five on Vietnam, and twenty added to China for about forty in total. Intended effect: treat subsidies as excess capacity and force negotiations. Risks: protectionists say overcapacity is real, notably in China; free-traders say import prices land on American consumers and invite retaliation; the EU says it is a victim, not a source, and the China truce expires in January. Read-through: more goods inflation for a Fed already facing a hot print, a firmer dollar, and pressure on Asia's tech supply chain. These are proposals, not law.

Fed. Inflation rose to three point eight percent, the highest since May, on energy. A December hike, at the meeting on the eighth and ninth, is widely expected and markets treat it as the base case; the argument is over size.

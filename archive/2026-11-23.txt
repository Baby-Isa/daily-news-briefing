Politics and government.

United States. Democrats have won at least two hundred fifty-eight House seats, their biggest majority since nineteen ninety-two, with four races undecided. Pediatrician Annie Andrews unseated Senator Graham in South Carolina. Trump says Schumer will be very hard to work with.

UK. Timms's review will call for a fundamental overhaul of disability benefit assessments, not scrapping the benefit for under-twenty-fives. Burnham tells MPs the EU deal could make the next decade much better; the Conservatives say they would scrap it. Poundland has days to find a buyer or enter administration.

Europe. Belgium's airports shut under an anti-austerity strike. Spain votes next Sunday.

East Africa. Ruto and Tshisekedi agreed in Oyo to restore full ambassador-level relations between Kenya and Congo. Kenya's electoral commission's Smartmatic contract is expiring, a question for twenty twenty-seven voter registration. Tanzania: nothing.

Conflict and security.

Iran. An Iranian strike on an industrial site in Bahrain wounded two; Saudi Arabia and Qatar condemned it. Iran's navy says two supertankers hit mines near Hormuz. Saudi strikes killed four in Yemen's Hodeida; the Houthis say they hit Saudi Arabia's Rabigh refinery.

Ukraine. A Russian strike collapsed part of an apartment block near Kyiv, killing at least nine. A Russian corvette docked in Benghazi, Libya, was sunk in a suspected Ukrainian sea drone attack; eastern Libya has complained to the United Nations.

Israel. Netanyahu's and Eisenkot's parties ended coalition talks with no deal. Eisenkot called settler arson on West Bank villages Jewish terrorism.

Sudan. Drones hit Khartoum homes and a United Nations building; the army blames Ethiopia.

Economy and markets.

AI trade. The mechanism is credit: lenders backing SpaceX's forty-three billion dollar lease of Nvidia chips worry the collateral could end up in orbit, beyond reach if Musk defaults. Intended effect: keep GPU demand financed. Risk: one school sees vendor financing as ordinary growth capital; another sees the dot-com telecom pattern of suppliers lending to their own customers. Read-through: if funding tightens, capex guidance and your largest holdings are exposed. The Financial Times has Wall Street set to rebound today.

Australia. Chalmers warns unemployment will pass five percent and growth stays under two percent in twenty twenty-seven and twenty twenty-eight. The New Zealand dollar is at a seventeen-year low against the dollar.

Oil. Prices hold a tight range as Gulf attacks sustain the risk premium.

Science and technology.

Europe's and Japan's BepiColombo entered Mercury orbit on Saturday, only the second mission ever to do so. A robot submarine in the Mariana Trench found unprecedented volcanic activity. SpaceX launched two rockets from one pad thirty-two hours apart, a record.

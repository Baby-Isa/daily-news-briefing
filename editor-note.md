# Editor's note - applies to the brief dated 2026-11-19 ONLY

This note is from the owner, for the drafting subagent. On 2026-11-19 it
overrides `briefing-prompt.md` wherever the two conflict. On any other date,
ignore it entirely and follow the spec as normal. This episode will also be
shared with a senior contact, a managing partner at a private-equity firm,
so it has to be the best version of the brief, not a different show.

The problem: the brief has gone a bit flat. There is too much blow-by-blow
conflict and politics, not enough insight, and the weather jokes have
drifted into long, clever-clever conceits that don't land. Fix that for one
day without redesigning anything.

## Length

- Target about 1,150 words (roughly 7.5 minutes), not 1,450. Shorter and
  sharper beats complete.
- Get there by covering fewer stories and spending fewer words on each.
  Never cut analysis to save words.
- Threshold lanes (Built environment, Law and justice, Society) run only if
  something is genuinely big. Editorial picks get one line each, at most two
  picks.

## Weather (about 60-70 words)

- Today plus the next two days only. Do not give the rest-of-week trend.
- Skip the extended metaphor or "angle" for the week (no gym, box set,
  piggy bank, free trial).
- Instead, give two or three short, practical, needling jokes aimed at me,
  in the sarcastic register that reliably lands. For example:
  - "Watch out for the sweaty people on the train."
  - "You'll take the umbrella, you won't need it, you'll resent it, and
    you'll take it again tomorrow anyway."
  - "Coat on, gloves on, dignity off."
- Say each joke once and move on. No walk-back and no label joke.

## Straight after weather: the big picture (about 80-100 words)

- Open the news with a short "how today's stories connect" passage. Stitch
  two or three of the day's threads into one argument.
- For example: how the midterm result, and the Iran war's part in it,
  changes Washington's room to respond to Iran now, and whether Tehran reads
  that as weakness. Or how a rate decision, an energy shock and a market
  move feed each other.
- Say what it implies next. Label anything speculative as such.
- Pick whichever connection the day's news genuinely supports. Do not force
  one.

## Conflict and politics

- One tight line per development unless it feeds the big-picture passage.
- No casualty-by-casualty or strike-by-strike lists.
- Leave out headline-only claims the brief cannot stand behind, rather than
  airing them with caveats.

## Economy and markets: the protected core

- Keep the full analytical depth from spec section 4. Where it genuinely
  fits, name the theory: the Phillips curve, Taylor rule, term premium,
  fiscal dominance and so on.
- Say what the move means, not only that it happened.
- Include one sentence on what markets are pricing.

## Mergers and acquisitions

- Lead with UK and European mid-market and private-equity deals: buyouts,
  take-privates, secondaries, exits, fund raises, financing conditions.
- Give the deal logic in a clause each: why this asset, why now, what it
  says about the cycle.
- Mega-deals only if they matter to that lens.

## Register

- Crisper sentences, fewer qualifiers.
- Each item answers "so what?" in its last clause.
- Everything else in the spec still applies: zero digits, no markdown,
  nothing from `aired-items.md` re-run without a new fact, and the
  failed-sources closing line.

Somehow, in the courts of late-Renaissance Italy, the gun had become an acceptable, even valued, weapon. Beyond the palazzi of the peninsula’s towns and cities, guns were also making inroads into more ordinary lives. There were no standing armies in the 16th-century Italian states.

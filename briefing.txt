Politics and government.

Conflict and security.

Yemen. The Houthis are about sixty kilometres from Aden, the government's interim seat, having taken two districts, per AFP. Government forces pulled back from the Bab el-Mandeb strait to defend Aden, ending a six-week Saudi-backed push. The Houthis also released six hundred detainees.

Iran. The Guards say two more tankers hit mines in Hormuz after taking the southern route they call illegal. A third US carrier has arrived and Iran warns it could strike first. Bahrain says its defences destroyed several Iranian drones aimed at a US base; Saudi Arabia condemned the attack.

Iraq. The prime minister is cracking down on oil smuggling in Jurf al Sakhar, a stronghold of Kataib Hezbollah, sacking officials and preparing a security move. The militia calls it blackmail to force disarmament.

Ukraine. Zelensky says Starshield limits are enabling Russian strikes on energy and railways. EU foreign ministers meet today to weigh Russia's maximalist demands from Abu Dhabi.

Israel. Eisenkot says Netanyahu must leave immediately over reports an aide interfered in the election. Four countries urged Israel to let in aid before winter.

Economy and markets.

Japan. The economy shrank for the first time in a year as weak consumer spending and record rain hit; the digest gave no figure. Mechanism: the Bank of Japan holds at one point two five percent, and dissenter Takata now urges a swift hike. Intended effect: stop yen weakness feeding inflation. Risk: tightening into a contraction. Hawks call the rain a one-off; doves say fragile demand makes it a mistake. Read-through: a hike narrows the dollar-yen gap, a risk to the carry trade that funds US tech.

China. October activity slowed more than expected as Beijing speeds up budget spending; no figures in the digest.

Nvidia. The Journal says Washington will name Nvidia and others national champions. The tension: industrial policy versus export control. Read-through: key-person and policy risk in your largest holding.

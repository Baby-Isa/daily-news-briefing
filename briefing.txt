Economy. The long end again, and it has moved from pressure to policy. Bank of Japan governor Ueda has hinted at a September hike, days after the ten-year Japanese government bond touched three percent and after Bessent publicly told Tokyo to raise rates.

The mechanism: the BoJ lifts its policy rate while its own bond market is already selling, so the tightening arrives through price and quantity together. The intended effect is a narrower rate gap that lifts the yen, importing disinflation and answering Washington without a tariff. The second-order risk is the one to hold onto: debt service rises on a stock above two hundred percent of output, and an unwinding yen carry trade forces Japanese institutions to repatriate and sell Treasuries, which is the buyer the US long end cannot afford to lose. Monetarists read the global move as inflation risk being repriced; the fiscal-dominance camp reads it as too much duration chasing too few buyers. Read-through: if it is term premium rising, the discount rate on long-duration AI capex names moves against you before earnings do. Yamaguchi Financial is already dumping Japanese bonds on faster hikes, which is that mechanism visible in one balance sheet.

Economy and markets.

Britain. Mechanism: Burnham will end the pension triple lock in twenty thirty, the rule lifting the state pension by the highest of earnings, inflation or two point five percent. Intended effect: fund the care service; earlier estimates put the lock's cost near fifteen and a half billion pounds by then. Risk: pensioners vote in the highest numbers; hawks call the lock unaffordable, others defend the floor. Read-through: a fiscal-discipline signal for gilts and sterling, but the twenty-eighth of October budget carries the detail; what replaces the lock was not in the digest.

America. Consumer confidence is at its lowest since twenty fourteen on inflation and stagnant wages, with crude back above one hundred dollars. That is stagflation: weak demand, sticky prices, a Fed that hiked in September. Keynesians say ease; monetarists say anchor expectations first.

The White House held crunch talks on a diesel export ban. Intended effect: cheaper pump prices before the midterms. Risk: nineteen seventies-style controls cut investment and lift prices over time.

The Court of International Trade hears the challenge to Trump's third global tariffs today: ten to twelve and a half percent on fifty-nine countries and the EU, after the Supreme Court struck down the earlier ones. Analysts expect these to fall too.

Mexico's exports rose nearly thirty percent through August to a record five hundred forty-nine billion dollars, led by computer equipment. China's research spending hit a record two point eight percent of output, above the OECD average for the first time.

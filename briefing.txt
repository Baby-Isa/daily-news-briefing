Economy. The Fed is back in its own story after two silent days. Governor Christopher Waller said he could support holding rates at the fifteenth-to-sixteenth September meeting if August inflation, published on the eleventh, keeps cooling, but would consider a hike if it does not. Implied odds of a September hike fell about twelve points to roughly fifty-five percent; yields fell, equities rallied. The mechanism: no lever moved, only guidance. One voting governor shifted the reaction function from hiking into decelerating inflation towards dependence on a single print. The intended effect: buy optionality without loosening financial conditions, so Warsh's two-percent pledge survives while the committee waits. The risk: that is credibility spent in advance. If August comes in hot, the Fed either hikes into a softening labour market or is seen to blink, and the term-premium problem we have tracked gets worse rather than better. Monetarists would read a pause as expectations unanchoring; the fiscal-dominance camp would say a hike does nothing about the supply of duration. Read-through: for the AI capital-spending names the discount rate matters more than the print. August payrolls land this afternoon and are the next input. Two, the long end.

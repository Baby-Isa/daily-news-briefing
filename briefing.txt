Economy. Tariffs first, because a court just widened them. The US Court of International Trade upheld the order killing the eight-hundred-dollar de minimis exemption, dismissing an auto-parts dealer's challenge.

The mechanism. Every low-value parcel entering the United States is now dutiable, closing the channel Chinese direct-to-consumer platforms used to ship duty-free.

The intent. Revenue, plus protection for domestic retailers against sellers whose entire cost advantage was that exemption.

The risk. This is a consumption tax with regressive incidence, landing hardest on low-income buyers, and it feeds goods inflation at the wrong moment. Economists split on who pays: the pass-through reading says consumer prices simply rise, the margin reading says exporters and platforms absorb part of it to hold share. The 2018 tariff evidence favoured near-full pass-through.

The read-through. Firmer goods prices argue for the Fed holding rather than cutting, against an equity market priced for cuts. Note the pattern with the drone tariffs we flagged: the administration is rebuilding tariff authority piece by piece, through Section 232 and now the courts, after the Supreme Court killed the original route in February.

Two. UK inflation. Wednesday's July print is expected close to three percent, driven by energy bills lifted by the Iran war. Expect a hold, and sterling soft on the growth read rather than firm on the inflation read.

As flagged, Warsh gives his first Jackson Hole speech as Fed chair on Friday, three weeks before the September meeting, with markets pricing roughly a one-in-three chance of a rate rise, not a cut. No new datapoint today, so take the Treasury buyback in four parts. Mechanism: Treasury is buying back its own long-dated bonds, funded at the short end, to push long yields down. Intent: compress the term premium and cheapen the financing of forty trillion of gross debt already costing over a trillion a year in interest. Risk: this is a maturity swap, not debt reduction. It shortens average maturity, so more of the stock reprices at whatever the front end does next, and if Warsh raises in September that cost lands immediately.

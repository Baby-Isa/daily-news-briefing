Politics and government.

Israel, result pending. Record turnout; exit polls leave neither bloc at sixty-one. Partial counts put Netanyahu's bloc near fifty-six, Eisenkot's in the forties, Arab-led parties around sixteen to nineteen, a record. Both men claim the right to govern. Netanyahu calls any coalition with Arab parties a disgrace. Official results take days.

Brazil: Flavio Bolsonaro left for the United States, defying a Supreme Court order barring him from leaving, with his passports cancelled. Washington will grant a visa. Lula calls it shameful.

United States, six days out: a judge deferred a ruling on California's noncitizen-voter ballots, saying the government has a remedy after the election.

UK: Budget at lunchtime. Minimum wage rises, but eighteen-to-twenty-year-olds get no bigger increase, which critics call a broken manifesto pledge. Fifteen million pounds for an anti-misogyny fund. A bank tax is expected. The King began a Caribbean tour, acknowledging slavery's suffering as reparations calls grow.

East Africa: Tanzania marks a year since the post-election killings; Amnesty says nobody has been held accountable. Jailed opposition leader Lissu is on hunger strike after his lawyers were barred.

Jordan and Syria reopened their border crossing and will form a joint security committee after Jordan's strikes. Sudan: drones reportedly killed over one hundred in a South Darfur market.

Economy and markets.

Fed decision at six this evening London time. Mechanism: the range stays at three and three-quarters to four percent unless the committee hikes again; a hold is the lean, December the live question. Intended effect: let September's hike work through while energy-driven inflation of three point seven percent is watched for second-round effects. Risk, two readings: Phillips-curve readers say hold, hike if expectations drift; supply-shock readers say tightening into an oil shock buys recession, not disinflation. Read-through: a hawkish tilt props the dollar and squeezes long-duration tech and Asian emerging markets. Warsh gives no forward guidance, so the press conference is the signal.

UK Budget: the FT says the chancellor's first task is to look prudent again, with borrowing costs climbing.

China's state-backed funds and brokers are buying stocks as investors leave the AI trade. SK Hynix beat on profit and will return seventy-five billion dollars to investors this year: memory is the toll booth of the AI build-out, which is why Korea's exposure cuts both ways. Jio listed twenty-one percent up after the record three point two billion dollar IPO.

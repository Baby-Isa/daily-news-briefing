Economy and markets.

United States. October consumer prices rose point four percent after point six, core point three, services excluding housing point four, nearly five and a half percent annualised over three months. The shutdown means no official annual figure; the New York Times calculates about four percent against three point seven. Governor Waller says a half-point hike in December is possible if core stays at point three or more. Mechanism: policy sits at three point seven five to four, a quarter-point rise expected December eighth to ninth, now with a double on the table. Intended effect: anchor expectations after nearly six years above target. Risk: Phillips-curve readers say tight demand needs this; supply-shock readers say diesel and food costs are beyond rates, and oil is already twenty dollars lower, so hiking into a data-centre slowdown over-tightens. Read-through: higher for longer weighs on long-duration tech multiples and props the dollar against sterling.

Nvidia. About four hundred thirty billion dollars wiped off its value in two days over the Huang affair. The board hired a former Manhattan federal prosecutor to investigate, shareholders sued claiming they were misled, and Commerce delayed adding Megaspeed to its trade blacklist. For the largest holding: key-person and export-control risk together. Huang's place at next week's APEC in Shenzhen is in doubt.

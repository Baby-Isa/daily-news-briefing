Economy and markets.

Fed. Mechanism: rates held at three and three-quarters to four percent, ten votes to two, both dissenters wanting a quarter-point hike, one being governor Lisa Cook. Warsh also cuts rate-setting meetings to seven next year and six in twenty twenty-eight. Intended effect: let September's hike and tighter credit do the work; he called conditions tighter but not tight. Risk, two readings: Phillips-curve economists, seeing inflation at three point seven percent, expect a December hike; supply-shock economists say oil drives prices, so tightening costs jobs without fixing supply. Fewer meetings also make each decision a bigger surprise. Read-through: December stays live, supporting the dollar and pressuring long-duration tech. For sterling, the Bank of England decides next Thursday; three of nine wanted a hike in September. The Bank of Japan decides today.

UK Budget: tax thresholds stay frozen, so inflation drags earners into higher bands; one percent departmental cuts fund defence. The OBR sees living standards up just one percent across the post-Covid decade.

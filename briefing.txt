Weather. Ruislip. Seventeen to twenty-three today, partly cloudy, five point four millimetres of rain. Warm and damp, so you'll sweat inside the coat you put on for the drizzle. Now. Tomorrow's forecast says clear sky. It also says eleven millimetres of rain. Eleven. That is not a clear sky, mate, that is a bucket. One process writes the adjective, another writes the number, and the two have never once spoken. Dress for the number. The number has nothing to sell you. After that it cools hard, sixteen by Thursday, seven degrees off today. The wettest day of your week is the one calling itself sunny.

Economy. As flagged all week, the Federal Reserve's rate-setting committee meets tomorrow and Wednesday, and the eve of it delivered a worse oil number, not a better one: Brent is near a hundred and eight dollars this morning against a hundred and four at Friday's close, on that pipeline closure. The mechanism. August consumer price inflation held at three point four per cent with core rising to two point four, failing Governor Waller's own recorded condition for a hold, and a quarter-point rise is about ninety per cent priced, an increase rather than a slower cut. The intent. Stop an energy shock leaking into wage and price expectations while the labour market is still adding jobs. The risk is where economists genuinely split. The Phillips-curve reading says a tight labour market turns a relative-price move into a wage-price spiral, so tighten now; the supply-shock reading says rates cannot drill oil, that hiking into a supply contraction tightens into weakness, and that core producer prices at plus zero point two against plus zero point three expected say upstream pressure is still narrow. The read-through. A hike firms the dollar, weakens sterling against it and lifts the discount rate on the artificial-intelligence capital spending trade, the largest exposure here. A hold gets read as capitulation to a president who said again yesterday that America should have the world's lowest rates, steepening the long end rather than calming it. A hike with dovish guidance, framed as one move and not a cycle, is what equities are positioned for, and the dot plot is where you check it. Nothing has been decided; watch the framing, not the number. Elsewhere, artificial-intelligence shares fell worldwide on the safety warnings, SoftBank down as much as thirteen per cent, that argument's first appearance in a price.

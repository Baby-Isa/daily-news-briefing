Weather.

Day eight of the dry run, and I am officially out of new ways to gloat about it: thirteen to twenty-three today, partly cloudy, not a drop, wind you will not even notice. Jumper for the morning, gone by lunchtime. Thursday is much the same, high of twenty-two. Friday is the peak of the whole run, twenty-five, with the faintest trace of rain that barely counts. Saturday cools to nineteen under cloud, still dry. Sunday is the one that actually breaks it — proper rain for the first time in well over a week, back down to twenty. I have got nothing clever left to say about any of this. It has been fine for eight days straight. Go outside.

Politics and government.

Trump's United Nations speech threatened to "annihilate" Iran without a deal, then revealed his own envoys had held direct talks with Tehran for the first time in months — most of Iran's delegation walked out mid-speech, then came back for the meeting anyway. Burnham, fresh off his first Trump meeting, got a mixed verdict: praised for warmer relations, but told to "reconsider" the Chagos handover; he raised the Falklands too, without reply. Russia's Duma election: real turnout was roughly forty percent against an official fifty-nine, analysts say, with tally sheets reportedly being rewritten on a large scale in Moscow and protests now planned in four cities.

Conflict and security.

Hormuz: Iran has given the US its conditions to reopen the strait, conveyed through Qatar to envoy Witkoff — an end to the war on all fronts, a halt to "acts of aggression," and unfrozen assets. Traffic through the strait fell to three vessels Tuesday against a ten-day average of fifteen; oil fell on the talks, Brent under ninety-nine dollars a barrel, its longest losing streak in over a year. Ukraine: Zelensky says he is ready for a bilateral energy ceasefire with Russia after meeting Trump, but also warns Moscow is planning a new mass attack on his country. Yemen: Houthis keep pushing into the highlands and striking Saudi Arabia; Riyadh has renewed its request for US air support.

International affairs.

Three disasters to flag: Typhoon Dujuan's Japan toll revised up to four dead, six missing; Hurricane Polo, now category five, closing schools in Mexico's Guerrero and Michoacán states. North Korea's missile tests this week look aimed at the Xi-Trump summit agenda, analysts say — pressure for recognition as a nuclear power before any talks with Washington. Kenya: Uhuru Kenyatta is doubling down on his claim that Raila Odinga actually won the twenty twenty-two election, promising to "speak up" further, as Ruto's allies reject it. Tanzania stays quiet, President Samia still on mourning leave.

Economy and markets.

Xi Jinping arrives in Washington today for Thursday's high-stakes trade summit, armed with rare-earth leverage Trump still has no answer to: Chinese magnet shipments to the US fell again in August, and the one-year trade truce expires in November with no breakthrough expected on rare earths, artificial intelligence or Taiwan. Diesel: Treasury Secretary Bessent is examining an export ban Trump says he backs, to bring down record US pump prices. Mechanism: restrict diesel leaving the country. Intended effect: keep more fuel at home. Risk: diesel is a globally priced, fungible commodity, so the oil industry argues a ban would do little for American drivers while further starving Europe, where the same Middle East disruption already has motorists paying two hundred and three million euros a day extra — forty percent more per tank than in January. Read-through: another twist in the energy-driven inflation feeding into the Bank of England's conditional rate hold and the Federal Reserve's own path. Britain borrowed eighteen billion pounds in August, more than forecast, adding to pressure on the chancellor ahead of the twenty-eighth of October budget — exactly the kind of shock International Monetary Fund chief Kristalina Georgieva says has pushed rich-country debt up "like a staircase, not to heaven."

Business and industry.

Ineos is mothballing three Hull chemical plants, billionaire owner Jim Ratcliffe blaming "ridiculously high" UK gas prices for making them uncompetitive — the same energy-cost squeeze hitting hauliers and taxi drivers as diesel sits at its highest since twenty twenty-two. DoorDash will pay a hundred and thirty-one point five million dollars over underpaying two hundred and sixty-four thousand New York delivery workers. And Europe's carmakers, already battling Chinese competition and Jaguar Land Rover's plan to cut four thousand more UK jobs, are hoping rearmament spending gives their factories a new customer.

Mergers, acquisitions and private equity.

Paramount's hundred-and-ten-billion-dollar Warner Brothers Discovery takeover has cleared its US states settlement, but now faces a bond-market test: the combined company emerges with around eighty billion dollars of net debt. And the US government and Gulf billionaires are backing financier Todd Boehly's bid for Lukoil's international assets, aiming to displace Carlyle, which struck its own deal for the sanctioned Russian group's overseas business back in January.

Science and technology.

Toyota is training its own workers to teach humanoid robots factory tasks, ahead of a plan for four hundred thousand of the machines on its production lines — the company insists humans will not be replaced, joining the industry-wide race to deploy humanoid labour at scale. And at the Large Hadron Collider, physicists have ruled out another hiding place for hypothetical quantum black holes, sharply narrowing where the extra spatial dimensions some theories predict could still be concealed.

Gadgets and consumer tech.

Qualcomm's new flagship mobile chips, the Snapdragon eight Elite Gen six and a faster Extreme Gen six variant, bring better AI processing, video capture and gaming performance; Motorola's Signature twenty-seven is first to use the Extreme chip, though its price and release date are still unannounced. Separately, China's CXMT has crossed a twelve-nanometre-class milestone in memory-chip manufacturing.

Energy and environment.

World leaders are set to adopt a UN declaration this week calling sea level rise an "existential threat" — oceans are now rising more than five times faster than they did across the twentieth century. And Texas has halted new data-centre permits, environmental scrutiny growing even among Governor Abbott's own Republicans.

Health and medicine.

The Trump administration is removing roughly seven hundred and sixty thousand people from Obamacare marketplaces over fraud allegations, a move Vice President Vance says saves two point two billion dollars. And America's Centers for Disease Control reports the suicide rate dipped slightly in twenty twenty-four, though officials say a sustained downward trend remains elusive.

Law and justice.

Days after banning three networks from the West Wing, the White House has launched its own twenty-four-hour "Trump TV" YouTube channel — a rival to the press access CNN, MS NOW and Politico are now suing to restore.

Culture and sport.

The head of world football's governing body FIFA, Gianni Infantino, has called for a review of football governance after backlash over the body's investment dealings — a rare admission of trouble at the top. The twenty twenty-six Booker Prize shortlist mixes former winners and debut authors, spanning historical fiction to campus novel to dystopia. And a six-year-old in China broke the women's world Rubik's Cube record twice in one day.

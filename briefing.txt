Weather. Today is the good one. Twelve up to twenty-two, cloudy, under a millimetre of rain. Go out, get the washing dry, because tomorrow you are going nowhere. Sunday brings fourteen and a half millimetres of rain at twenty-two degrees. Warm rain. Twenty-two degrees of it. That is not weather, that is being licked by a dog the size of a house. And the forecast has spent the week haggling itself down, seventeen, then sixteen, now fourteen and a half, like a man talking down his own offer at an auction he is the only bidder at. From Monday it dries and cools, so the week is fine. It is just tomorrow that is ruined.

Inflation, and the number we flagged. As promised, August American consumer prices landed after yesterday's brief was recorded, and they did not cool. Headline three point four percent year on year, unchanged from July. Core inflation, which strips out food and energy, rose to two point four percent. Market-implied odds of a rate rise at next week's Federal Reserve meeting jumped to about ninety percent.

The mechanism. A quarter-point rise in the federal funds target, tightening into an energy shock rather than a demand boom. The intent. Stop a relative-price move in oil generalising into wages, working through expectations rather than by crushing demand. The risk is where economists divide. The Phillips-curve reading says a labour market still adding a hundred and sixty-two thousand jobs a month will convert the shock into wage growth, so move now. The supply-shock reading says interest rates cannot drill for oil, and tightening into a fuel squeeze buys weakness without touching the cause, and Thursday's core producer prices, up zero point two against zero point three expected, say upstream pressure is narrow, not broad. Core consumer prices rising cuts the other way, which is why this is not settled. The read-through. Governor Waller's stated condition was a hold if this print cooled and a possible hike if it did not, and it did not, so the dovish case has lost. Expect a firmer dollar, weaker sterling against it, higher discount rates on the long-duration equity that is the artificial-intelligence capital-expenditure trade, and rising financing costs into the largest data-centre build ever attempted. The decision lands Tuesday and Wednesday, the fifteenth and sixteenth of September.

Conflict. One. The Iran war has taken the second waterway. Houthi forces seized Perim island in the Bab el-Mandeb strait and now hold the Yemeni Red Sea coast, and Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline, the route built specifically to bypass Hormuz, after a drone strike launched from Iraqi territory. Baghdad sacked its Maysan commander and closed the Shalamcheh crossing with Iran. Riyadh has not retaliated. Washington has cut tanker protection through Hormuz to two fixed windows a day after Iranian night attacks, and transits are in single digits. Both Gulf export routes are under fire at once, and that, not sentiment, holds crude above a hundred dollars. Two. Brussels released six point one billion euros of its Ukraine loan for Patriot interceptors, dropping the European-made-only condition. Russia struck Kyiv petrol stations with jet-powered drones for the first time.

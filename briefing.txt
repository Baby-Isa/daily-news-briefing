Turning to conflict and security, the dominant overnight story is still the standoff around the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway between Iran and Oman through which a large share of the world's seaborne oil passes, sitting downstream of the continuing US-Israeli war footing against Iran. Iran has now set formal conditions for reopening the strait, including compensation for war damage, even as its foreign minister said talks with Oman were nearing a final stage; the US vice president says Washington is making progress in direct talks with Tehran but wants long-term changes, not just a pause, before things properly ease. Complicating that, the United Arab Emirates says Iran struck one of its state oil company's tankers with a missile inside the strait, which Iran denies, and a shipping-tracking firm reports more than fifty tankers are now moving oil through the strait "covertly" — effectively an open shadow fleet. Worth being precise about the mechanism here: a prolonged closure or informal blockade of Hormuz pushes up global oil prices and shipping-insurance costs regardless of what headline crude benchmarks do day to day, which is the kind of driver worth tracking rather than any single day's price move.

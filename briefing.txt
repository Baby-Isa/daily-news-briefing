Politics and government.

Economy and markets.

Stocks and the AI trade. The Financial Times: global shares slid on Democratic wins, as traders bet a Democratic Congress slows the infrastructure build-out behind growth. Japan's Nikkei fell as much as four point eight percent, chip and AI shares down over thirteen. Wall Street, by contrast, recovered Tuesday afternoon from an AI-spending selloff, and the Guardian saw little midterm effect. Hold both open.

Two readings. Gridlock view: a divided Congress cannot pass new spending or tariff law, removing fiscal impulse but also tail risk. Oversight view: subpoenas and scrutiny of data-centre and chip deals. The practical lever is permitting: Senate leaders have teed up a reform vote after the midterms, and that governs how fast data centres get power.

Supplier demand is not wobbling: AMD posted record thirteen point four billion dollar quarterly revenue and guided to seventeen billion next quarter on data centres. Oracle cancelled a seven billion dollar Tencent AI deal under Trump pressure, so US infrastructure groups are squeezed between Washington and Chinese partners.

China: core dividend growth was five point three percent, lagging global, casting doubt on Beijing's stock-stabilisation plan; Chinese AI shares are slumping too. Vietnam has overtaken Indonesia on per-person output. Kenya's NCBA cut its growth forecast to four point eight percent.

Economy. One, and the date we flagged has arrived: Canada's retaliation took effect this morning. Mechanism: tariffs on about twenty billion dollars of American goods, up to fifty percent on steel, with fifty percent on cars, trucks and parts from the first of January, imposed after talks failed. Intent: targeted pain in politically exposed American sectors to force removal of the Section 232 metals and vehicle duties, the only lever a smaller economy has. Risk: an integrated supply chain turns that lever on its owner, since Canada is taxing its own manufacturing inputs, and Trump answered within hours by threatening to bar Bombardier from selling aircraft in America unless it moves production there, which is escalation, not bargaining. Read-through: mildly stagflationary both sides of the border, keeping the tariff channel alive in Friday's inflation print, the one the Federal Reserve meets next week to argue about. Two. China's exports surged again in August, pointing to a record annual surplus, days before Trump and Xi meet on extending their tariff truce. Three. Japan: wages grew at their fastest since nineteen ninety-seven and growth was revised up, which is the Bank of Japan's case for a hike, and the yen reclaimed a hundred and fifty-three. As flagged, the burden is shifting from finite intervention to rates. Four.

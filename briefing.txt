Economy. One. UK inflation, the print we flagged for today. Consumer prices rose two point nine percent in the year to July, up from two point six, the highest since March, driven by the Ofgem cap rising thirteen percent on the first of July, two hundred and twenty-one pounds onto the typical dual-fuel bill. That is an imported energy supply shock, not demand. It lifts the price level and cuts real income at the same time, which is the trade-off the Bank cannot hedge: tighten and you deepen the squeeze, hold and you risk second-round wage effects. Bank Rate stayed at three point seven five on the thirtieth of July, six to three, three members voting to raise. Hawkish dissent is growing even as vacancies hit a five-year low, so September looks like another hold, and the labour data does not yet support the second-round fear. Two. Tariffs. Trump paused the fifty percent tariff on Canadian goods two hours before it took effect, for three days. Mechanism: deferral, not cancellation. Intent: keep Carney negotiating without eating the price shock before the midterms. Risk: a rolling deadline delivers the tariff's real cost anyway, which is investment paralysis, whether the rate lands or not. Read-through: tariff policy is now episodic rather than scheduled, and that uncertainty is one reason term premia keep rising. Three. Long borrowing costs hit fresh multi-decade highs in the US, UK, Germany, France and Japan, Japanese yields near three percent. Drivers: Brent above ninety dollars for the first time since 30 July, heavy AI-related issuance, fiscal supply.

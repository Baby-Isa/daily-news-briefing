Economy. Gross federal debt has passed forty trillion dollars, doubled in a decade, with interest alone above a trillion a year. The mechanism: Treasury has doubled liquidity-support buybacks in the ten-to-thirty-year sector from two billion to at least four billion per operation, running the ninth of September to the fourth of November, after thirty-year yields hit five point two percent and the ten-year four point six five. The intent is to absorb duration and cap the long end without asking the Fed to do it, holding down mortgage and corporate borrowing costs. The risk is that it fails and advertises the problem: yields fell nine and six basis points and gave it back inside a day, Wall Street calls it a band-aid on a bullet hole, and Obstfeld at the Peterson Institute calls it spitting in a bucket. The benign reading is routine market plumbing; the hostile one is fiscal dominance, the Treasury managing the curve while the Fed is split, which raises the term premium it is trying to compress. Read-through: minutes show many Fed officials flagging inflation risk and a possible need to raise rates, not cut, so the discount rate on the AI capital expenditure cycle rises exactly as the hyperscalers lever up for it.

Economy and markets.

Xi Jinping's Washington summit: mechanism — Bessent and Vice Premier He Lifeng agreed Wednesday to extend the US-China trade truce two months, to the tenth of January, rather than let it lapse in November, as Trump and Xi met on US soil for the first time in over a decade. Intended effect: keep tariffs paused while negotiators hunt a bigger deal on chips, artificial intelligence and Taiwan — Bessent himself says he does not know if one is reachable. Risk: two months, not a year, signals trust remains thin, and pushes the real cliff-edge — rare-earth export curbs, chip restrictions — into January rather than removing it. Read-through: continued uncertainty for Nvidia and the Taiwan supply chain, the portfolio's largest single-name concentration, through the turn of the year.

Separately: bond yields are rising and oil holds above one hundred dollars a barrel, with markets pricing roughly seventy percent odds of a Federal Reserve rate rise in October — a hike, not a cut, driven by Middle East energy inflation. Competing readings: hike and choke growth already strained by climbing public debt costs, or let an energy-driven price shock run and risk it turning self-fulfilling — the same split between monetarists and supply-siders since the nineteen-seventies oil shocks. Three international bodies, the IMF among them, jointly warned this week that UK and US borrowing costs are becoming a shared risk. Emerging-market borrowers are meanwhile raising record sums abroad despite the Iran war and a firm dollar, and the Financial Times reports frontier AI labs and data-centre builders are delaying stock listings — worth watching against Anthropic's own mooted mid-October flotation, terms still awaited.

Science and technology.

Anthropic says its Claude model has independently discovered a CRISPR-like gene-editing enzyme system — one of the first instances of an AI model making a genuine biological discovery, not just accelerating existing research, feeding the live debate over whether AI capability is outrunning safety oversight. Separately, sediment records show California's Cascadia and northern San Andreas faults may sometimes rupture minutes to hours apart, raising the possibility that "the big one" on the US West Coast would not strike alone.

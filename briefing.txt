Economy and markets.

UK. Inflation rose to three point five percent from three point one, above forecasts; September borrowing hit twenty-four point five billion pounds, a year high. Phillips curve: falling unemployment plus accelerating prices leave the Bank of England no case to ease, supportive of sterling. Fiscally, Healey's hole widens before next Wednesday's Budget, with wholesale gas at its highest since twenty twenty-two.

France. Ten-year yields passed five percent for the first time in twenty-four years, on riots and a stalled budget. One reading: a political premium that fades once a budget passes. The other: a credibility problem, since higher interest costs deepen the deficit that spooked buyers.

South Africa. The mid-term budget cuts fuel levies by ten billion rand. Mechanism: revenue overshoot funds it. Intended effect: lower pump prices and inflation. Risk: spending the windfall as El Nino readiness needs money; the opposition calls it a scam. Read-through: rand-friendly disinflation, thinner cushion.

Japan: most Bank of Japan watchers expect the next hike in December. India: Trump says a trade deal is pretty close.

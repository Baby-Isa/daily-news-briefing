Economy. Tariffs, and this one has a real mechanism. One, mechanism. A hundred percent tariff on drones over twenty-five kilograms and on thermal-imaging drones, twenty-five percent on small ones, fifteen on parts from the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, ten from Britain, all under Section 232, the national-security route, chosen because the Supreme Court killed the earlier authority in February. Two, intent. Price Chinese airframes out and force a domestic supply chain, on a legal footing that survives challenge. Three, risks. The parts tariff taxes the American assemblers it is meant to protect. Infant-industry logic says protection buys time to scale; standard incidence says the cost lands on US buyers within a quarter while capacity takes years. Allies taxed at fifteen makes retaliation live. Four, read-through. Small for headline inflation, but Section 232 signals the tariff regime is durable, a permanent cost line for anyone building hardware, data centres included.

Economy and markets.

AI trade. Wall Street lost about three point two trillion dollars Thursday, the Nasdaq one hundred down six point four percent. The cause is political: Democratic control of Congress and forty-five local votes restricting data centres raise the odds of curbs on AI build-out. The Nikkei fell over seven percent, below sixty thousand for the first time since May; Seoul's Kospi fell under five thousand. Reading one: slower build means fewer orders for Nvidia and memory makers, so capex-driven earnings get repriced. Reading two, the Financial Times: Congress changes little, since Trump keeps executive power and permitting is local, so this overshoots. OpenAI pledged no more non-disclosure agreements with officials on data centres. Payrolls land this afternoon.

Bank of England. Mechanism: Bank Rate up a quarter point to four percent, the first rise in over three years; the governor signals another soon. Intended effect: stop an energy shock hardening into wages, Phillips curve logic, with the Bank warning inflation near five percent next year. Risk: it is a supply shock rates cannot fix, so mortgage holders pay for no price relief. Read-through: the BBC's Faisal Islam expects one more rise, then a stop; sterling, at its strongest since the Brexit vote, gets rate support against a dollar that weakened after the midterms.

Elsewhere: developing-country debt hit a record nine point four trillion dollars, the poorest paying the highest interest since the late nineteen eighties. Mexico's US trade surplus jumped twenty-six percent to a record one hundred eighty-four billion dollars in nine months, on AI hardware.

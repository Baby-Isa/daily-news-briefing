Economy and markets.

Bonds. As flagged, the sell-off is spreading. The euro hit a seventeen-month low on French debt fears; British five-year fixed mortgages averaged six percent, the first time in three years; junk-rated US borrowers now feel the Treasury rout. Mechanism: a higher term premium lifts every long rate. Risk: refinancing walls, and long-duration tech valuations. Bank of Japan sources say it may signal underlying inflation has reached two percent, a further pull on global duration.

France. Nationwide strikes today as student protests spread, nearly two hundred pupils injured in clashes. The budget must cut the deficit; the street resists; a censure motion is the veto. A Spanish election adds periphery risk.

Fuel. Trump eased limits on red diesel, the untaxed dyed fuel, to cool fuel inflation, calling high gas prices a small price for safety. Intended effect: more usable supply. Risk: it shifts fuel around without adding refining capacity, and China reportedly suspended October fuel exports, squeezing Asia's diesel. OPEC plus holds output steady.

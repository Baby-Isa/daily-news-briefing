Economy and markets.

United States. Brent hit a five-month high of one hundred ten dollars after Saudi-led forces intercepted missiles aimed at energy hubs. Big Tech had its worst week since the April tariff shock, and money fled into Treasuries, halting the months-long bond sell-off. That is an unplanned easing: falling long yields loosen conditions just as three point seven percent inflation and oil argue for the Fed hiking again on the twenty-seventh to twenty-eighth October. Monetarists say a Fed that tolerates this lets expectations drift; Keynesians say rates cannot cure a supply shock and the equity hit will slow demand anyway. Meanwhile credit tightens where it matters for the AI cycle: Anthropic is offering Blackstone a double-digit yield on chip financing, complicating its investment-grade goal. That is the real price of capex now.

Bangkok: hundreds protested the IMF's austerity call to developing economies. Pakistan takes the chair of both IMF and World Bank governing boards, a first in seven decades.

Economy. The global bond sell-off again, now with a named driver: oil-driven inflation expectations stacked on debt-funded artificial intelligence construction.

The mechanism. No central bank decided anything; the term premium moved. Investors want more yield to hold long-dated paper, with Brent above ninety feeding headline inflation and hyperscaler capital spending arriving as fresh issuance. The intended effect, from the treasuries borrowing, is that they get the money regardless: deficits are legislated, so supply is inelastic. The second-order risk splits the profession. Monetarists read a rising long end as inflation expectations coming unanchored, arguing for tighter policy. The fiscal-dominance camp reads it as too much duration chasing too few buyers, where tightening worsens debt service and pushes the yield higher still. Read-through: if this is term premium rather than growth, the discount rate on long-duration AI names rises before their earnings move at all, and the same cash flows are simply worth less.

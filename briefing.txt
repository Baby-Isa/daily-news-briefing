Conflict and security.

One. The war has opened a second waterway. Houthi forces seized the Yemeni port of Mokha and advanced down the Red Sea coast to strategic islands, about eighty kilometres from Bab el-Mandeb, through which some twelve percent of global trade passes, and in range of Saudi Red Sea exports. The Hormuz blockade replicated at the other end of the route, with Hormuz transits themselves into single digits a day. Two. Gulf foreign ministers meet Iran's Araghchi in Oman to push a temporary shipping deal as a path to reopening the strait, so a diplomatic channel is live. Three. The Lebanese army claims operational control of three southern pilot zones, but eighty-six percent of its weapons seizures there were light ammunition, so the disarmament gap the June framework exists to close is still open. Four. Canada pledged two hundred and fifty-four million dollars of air defence to Kyiv as Ukrainian sea drones struck Sochi.

Economy and markets.

One. American consumer price inflation for August lands at half past one this afternoon, after this recording. As flagged all week, this is the print next week's Federal Reserve meeting turns on. Expected: three point four percent on the year, four tenths on the month. Yesterday's producer prices give the shape: five point four percent annually, a tenth hot, diesel up twenty-four percent doing nearly all of it, core up two tenths against three expected. Upstream pressure is narrow, not broad.

The mechanism is Governor Waller's stated condition: hold on the fifteenth and sixteenth of September if today's print cools, hike if it does not, and futures price the hike. The intent is to stop an oil shock hardening into inflation expectations before the committee sits Tuesday and Wednesday. The Phillips-curve reading says a labour market this tight, a hundred and sixty-two thousand payrolls against a fifty-three thousand consensus and unemployment at four point one, generalises any price shock, so tighten; the supply-shock reading says this is a relative price move in energy, that yesterday's soft core confirms it, and that tightening into it buys a recession and no disinflation. The read-through: a hot headline with a soft core is the worst combination for you, because it delivers a hike into a supply shock, lifts the dollar against sterling, and raises the discount rate on the artificial-intelligence capital-expenditure trade, where your concentration sits.

Two. The long end has not healed. The Treasury's six-billion-dollar buyback, flagged yesterday, failed to break the fever: ten-year yields sit near three-year highs and the bond sell-off resumed as crude jumped six percent above a hundred and seven dollars. Three. The European Central Bank raised its rate to two and a half percent, warning eurozone price pressures from the war will last longer than expected, and the Bank of Japan lifts next week. Three central banks tightening into one barrel of oil is the thing to hold.

Business and industry.

American export controls are visibly building the competitor they were meant to prevent. Biren, a Chinese accelerator maker, posted two-thousand-percent annual revenue growth as Nvidia and AMD exit that market, and rival Enflame closed one hundred and eighty-eight percent up on its Shanghai debut. Demand that was Nvidia's is being permanently re-domiciled: the second-order cost of the policy.

Nestlé is selling its Nature's Bounty vitamins platform to Yellow Wood for a billion dollars, having paid five and three-quarter billion for that supplements business in twenty twenty-one: a very large write-down of the consumer-health diversification thesis. Science and technology.

One. Anthropic published the most concrete account yet of frontier-model misuse: state-linked surveillance run from China and Iran against minorities and dissidents, five cases of researchers circumventing controls to advance biological weapons work, and a system built to target voters in all two hundred and twenty-two Malaysian constituencies. It also alleges Chinese labs Moonshot and DeepSeek secretly routed user requests to its models, a distillation claim. Two. A catalogue of nearly three thousand supernovae indicates dark energy is changing over time rather than holding constant, which would force a revision of the standard model of cosmology.

Energy and environment.

The war has pushed global coal consumption to a record, five years after almost every government agreed to phase it down. And Britain has conceded aviation emissions in twenty fifty will run about fifty percent above its jet-zero target, twenty-eight million tonnes against nineteen, having quietly cut its assumptions for sustainable fuels and electric aircraft just before approving a third Heathrow runway.

Health and medicine.

Platinum chemotherapy given in childhood permanently ages liver cells, making them resemble adult tissue and possibly raising long-term tumour risk, which strengthens the case for lifelong follow-up of childhood cancer survivors.

On Japan: the Bank of Japan's July meeting summary flagged upside risks to prices and signalled a possible rate rise as soon as September. The mechanism is straightforward — Tokyo has held its policy rate at just one percent through a period of persistent yen weakness that's been pushing up import costs and, cumulatively, inflation; a hike would tighten that directly. The risk cuts both ways: move too slowly and the weak yen keeps feeding through to prices — Japanese executives are already publicly calling for currency stability — but move too fast, given Japan just posted its first current-account deficit in a year and a half, and the central bank could tip that fragile recovery into contraction.

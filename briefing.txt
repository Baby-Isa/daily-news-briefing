Economy. The lead is a fiscal-monetary collision. The mechanism: the Treasury under Bessent is stepping up purchases of its own outstanding debt, concentrated at the long end, so a fiscal actor is now managing duration while the Fed is trying to tighten. The intent: strip duration out of the market, hold long yields down and contain an interest bill already above a trillion dollars a year. The risks split cleanly. One reading is benign plumbing, liquidity support for illiquid off-the-run bonds. The other is fiscal dominance, where debt management quietly overrides the inflation target; Bessent's own former mentor says publicly he will lose this fight with the bond market. If investors read buying as monetisation, term premium rises and the intervention defeats itself. The read-through: this undercuts Warsh three days before he speaks. As flagged, his first Jackson Hole address as chair is Friday, with markets pricing roughly a one-in-three chance of a September rate rise. A rise into a Treasury bidding for its own long end is the sharpest institutional conflict since the 1951 Accord, and for a dollar-denominated book it argues for higher term premium, a softer dollar, and a higher discount rate on AI capital spending.

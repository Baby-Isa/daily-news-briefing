Conflict and security.

Hormuz: Iran has offered a new seven-day ceasefire proposal that its foreign minister says could reopen the strait within a week; transits fell to just nine vessels Thursday against a ten-day average near eighteen, and US and Iranian negotiators are separately exploring a phased deal trading a reopening for an end to Washington's economic blockade. Ukraine: Zelensky says the "Anchorage" concessions Russia once sought are no longer on the table, crediting Ukraine's battlefield position; Macron says France sees signs Russia is preparing to mobilise three hundred thousand more men. Ethiopia: Tigrayan forces now call this a full-blown war; the army says it has repelled attacks in its first public comment, and the president blamed "external actors" at the UN, as Mekelle reportedly runs out of cash.

Economy and markets.

Mechanism: Freddie Mac says the average US mortgage rate has topped seven percent for the first time in twenty months, as the Federal Reserve's recent rate rise works through the system. Intended effect: the Fed is trying to choke off energy-driven inflation before it embeds into wage and price expectations, even at cost to growth. Risk: mortgage rates this high freeze an already supply-starved housing market and hit poorer, older borrowers hardest — economists split, in the old Phillips-curve argument, over whether an energy-driven shock like this should be treated like ordinary demand inflation, or left to burn through. Read-through: three international bodies, the IMF among them, warned this week that UK and US borrowing costs are becoming a shared risk, and higher-for-longer US rates keep the dollar firm against sterling while squeezing emerging-market borrowers.

Trump and Xi's Washington summit produced, as flagged, atmosphere over substance: no deal on tariffs, rare earths or soybeans, only Wednesday's two-month extension of the trade truce to the tenth of January. China's overseas investment hit a record two hundred fourteen billion dollars this year even as the flows specifically bound for the US plunge — decoupling continuing underneath the handshakes. Separately, a cheaper challenger model called Jev, from startup TypeSafe AI, is pitched directly at OpenAI and Anthropic's developer business — one more sign of margin pressure reaching the AI capex cycle.

Business and industry.

Costco's digital sales hit thirty-three billion dollars this year, up more than twenty percent, as delivery partnerships with DoorDash and Uber Eats pull in shoppers beyond the warehouse floor. Amazon is opening its Seller Central tool so merchants can run their eBay, Shopify, TikTok Shop and Walmart stores from inside Amazon's own system, deepening its grip on small-seller logistics regardless of where people actually buy. EBay has banned the sale of airbags after a rise in fake and faulty aftermarket parts turning up in cars.

Mergers, acquisitions and private equity.

Uber's fourteen point eight billion dollar bid for Delivery Hero looks set to complete: the German food-delivery group's own chief executive has cancelled his planned exit now that the takeover is secured, consolidating two of the sector's biggest names under one owner. GoDaddy shares jumped eleven percent on a report that Norton-maker Gen Digital has made an early approach for the web-hosting giant, though talks remain preliminary.

Science and technology.

Google's experimental Suncatcher satellite launches October the first: an orbital AI data centre running four processors in short bursts, the first real test of a bet that AI computing could eventually move into orbit. Separately, astronomers say a dead white dwarf star with no visible disk has somehow driven a shock wave that has lasted at least a thousand years — its magnetic field may explain part of it, but researchers admit an unidentified "mystery engine" may also be at work.

Gadgets and consumer tech.

Meta's third-generation Ray-Ban camera glasses start at four hundred forty-nine dollars, up from last year's line, buying an hour more battery life, two new frame styles and Dolby Atmos audio recording — the price rise is the real news, since the camera itself barely changes. Qualcomm's new Snapdragon Sound Elite Gen Two chip builds Wi-Fi radio straight into earbuds and audio glasses, the unglamorous kind of chip that decides whether next year's wireless audio finally stops cutting out.

Energy and environment.

UK diesel prices have jumped thirty-eight percent since February's attacks on Iran began, to close to two pounds a litre, and petrol is following, driven by Brent crude holding above one hundred dollars. Charging an electric car overnight now costs roughly a ninth as much per mile as filling a diesel tank, Carbon Brief calculates — the widest that gap has ever been.

Health and medicine.

Kyverna Therapeutics says new data show its cell therapy keeps autoimmune patients off other treatments long-term, moving it a step closer to being the first approved cell therapy for an autoimmune disease rather than only cancer. Separately, the CDC has finally opened state ordering for COVID-nineteen vaccines after an unexplained delay blamed on stalled procurement decisions.

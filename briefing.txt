Weather. Ruislip today. Eleven this morning, twenty-one this afternoon, fair, zero rain, wind that couldn't move a crisp packet. Eleven to twenty-one is the worst kind of day, because you dress for eleven, you commit, and by two you're carrying a coat around like a punishment. Rest of the week, dry and climbing, mid-twenties from Tuesday, and the nights stop cooling. Eighteen degrees overnight Thursday, which is not a night, that's a warm bath you can't get out of. One day breaks it. Wednesday. Rain, three millimetres, roughly one good sneeze, then done. Deodorant. That's the message.

Lead. The Canada deadline we flagged expired without a deal. Fifty percent tariffs took effect this morning on twenty billion dollars of Canadian goods, five percent of what Canada ships south: plywood, liquor, electrical equipment, hockey gear. The reported landing zone, steel and aluminium cut to twenty-five percent and autos to fifteen, did not happen. Carney will match dollar for dollar. Mechanism, a targeted Section 232 list, not the whole trade relationship. Intent, make a narrow slice of exports uneconomic to force concessions while sparing integrated auto and energy flows. Risk, matching turns a lever into a two-way tax paid by importers both sides, and puts the North American trade pact's renewal openly in doubt. Read-through, small for headline inflation at this size, large for the credibility of every other sector deal queued behind it.

Conflict. One. Iran. Treasury Secretary Bessent details the sanctions package Monday. Tehran calls it a declaration of war on all nations. A double-tap drone strike on Kryvyi Rih's largest shopping centre killed sixteen and wounded over a hundred and thirty, the second wave timed for rescue crews. The load-bearing fact underneath it: Ukraine has run out of American Patriot interceptors for ballistic defence. Kallas is threatening the harshest sanctions yet, and Gelendzhik's last petrol station is dry.

Economy. The Treasury buyback we flagged has failed its test. Stocks and bonds sold off together on Thursday, yields gave back the whole improvement, and gross debt sits at forty trillion. Competing readings: benign market plumbing, versus fiscal dominance, the state leaning on the bond market because the fiscal path won't bend. The second is gaining, and the latest Fed minutes had officials flagging a possible need to raise rates, not cut, which is the opposite of what an equity market priced for easing wants. Add mortgages at six point seven percent and diesel at five dollars, both war-driven, and the consumer channel tightens while the fiscal one loosens. Britain. July borrowing was one point eight billion in a month that normally runs a surplus, leaving the year to date two point three billion above the Office for Budget Responsibility forecast, ahead of Healey's first Budget on the twenty-eighth of October. Retail sales fell despite the heat. Mexico is holding rates despite a strong peso, pushing its three percent inflation target to late 2027.

Economy and markets.

The Federal Reserve, the Bank of Japan and the yen carry trade. Mechanism: the Bank of Japan has pushed policy to a thirty-one-year high and is read as still under-delivering, so Japan's ten-year yield, already above three percent, keeps climbing against an American ten-year near five, itself a nineteen-year high. Intended effect: defending a weak yen and choking off imported inflation. Risk: years of cheap-yen-funded bets into dollar assets now face a rising cost of the currency they're borrowed in, and a disorderly unwind is the classic trigger behind past global selloffs. Read-through: it hits the artificial intelligence trade directly — SoftBank alone is raising upward of eleven billion dollars in junk bonds against its OpenAI stake, so tighter yen funding squeezes exactly the leverage propping up the capital-expenditure cycle, even as the Federal Reserve still has further hikes pencilled in. Separately: India's National Stock Exchange is finally listing, a forty-six-billion-dollar debut years in the delay. Trump and Xi meet this week with the trade truce still uneasy — China's promised farm-goods purchases haven't materialised and rare-earth friction persists into the summit. And a Kremlin-linked forgery scheme moved six-point-nine billion dollars through Standard Chartered, Citigroup and other global banks, a fintech leak reveals.

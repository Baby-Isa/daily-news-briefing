Conflict. One, Hormuz. Iran is drawing up conditions for reopening the strait, ending the war among them, after Qatari mediation, and says it has agreed a corridor with Oman. Against that, transits collapsed to five vessels Thursday from seventeen Wednesday, ten-day average fifteen. Bessent says the blockade will crush Iran's economy and will press G20 finance ministers next week to cut Tehran's remaining lifelines; Trump says Washington is not talking to Iran at all. Two, Ukraine. Another overnight missile wave on Kyiv. The material point: US Patriot stocks in Europe are beyond critical, drained by the Iran war, with very limited ability to stop even one strike, and Russia is threatening British military targets over missile technology for Kyiv. Three, Syria. Israeli shells hit western Damascus and troops raided a Quneitra village, while al-Sharaa made the dissolved SDF's commander Mazloum Abdi a presidential adviser, the integration deal made real.

Economy. Warsh. This brief pre-dates the speech, which he gives at Jackson Hole later today, so what follows is what is expected, not what he said. The mechanism: he has already stripped forward guidance from the statement and rate forecasts from the dot plot, so the speech is the only guidance instrument he has left, three weeks before a September meeting markets price at roughly one-in-three for a rise. The intent is to re-anchor inflation expectations on credibility rather than on promises. The risk is where the schools split: one says removing guidance restores optionality and ends pre-commitment, the other that silence widens the term premium instead of lowering expectations, and July's data cuts against a hike anyway, core prices up nought point two percent on the month against nought point four in June. The read-through: hawkish means a steeper curve, a firmer dollar against sterling, and a higher discount rate on the very capital spending Nvidia validated on Wednesday; dovish is the reverse. Asian equities rose into it as the Nvidia euphoria faded. Two. US pre-tax corporate profits have hit their highest share of the economy since just after the war while employee compensation slides, so margins, not wages, are carrying the price level. That weakens the wage-driven Phillips-curve read Warsh would need to justify tightening. Three. Zero-coupon convertible issuance is on course for a record year, companies monetising AI-stock volatility to borrow at nothing, which tells you how the capex cycle is being financed.

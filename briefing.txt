Economy and markets.

Rates. India raised its repo rate twenty-five basis points to five and a half percent, the first hike since twenty twenty-three, and moved to calibrated tightening. Intended: pull inflation, four point eight percent in August against a four percent target, back down and defend a weak rupee against dear oil. Risk: this is a supply shock; hawks say it anchors expectations, others say hiking against imported fuel and a failed monsoon costs growth for little disinflation. Read-through: another central bank tightening into an oil shock; the global rate cycle is still turning up.

Bonds. Pimco warns the US ten-year yield could reach six percent, first since two thousand, as investors unwind losing bets; American mortgage rates hit seven point four percent. The Bank of England governor urges John Healey to use the twenty-eighth of October budget to calm bond fears. Deficits plus hawkish central banks lift term premia, with France the weak link.

AI capex. Financing is fraying while demand holds.

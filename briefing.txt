Politics and government.

First brief since Friday, so weekend results are flagged. Serbia, Sunday: the opposition Student List won an outright majority, fifty-two and a half percent, on the highest turnout in thirty years. Vucic conceded, blamed foreign interference without evidence, and said the winners would sanction Russia; they say otherwise. Brazil, Sunday: Lula won a fourth term, fifty-one point eight to forty-eight point two. Flavio Bolsonaro first refused to concede over a two-hour voting extension; the election court's president, a Bolsonaro appointee, rejected the challenge, and his party now accepts the result. Trump called it another rigged election. Israel votes today. Polls show neither bloc reaching sixty-one seats, with Arab-led parties potential kingmakers.

United States, seven days out: Trump offers a one million dollar reward for reporting voter fraud. A court ruled against the administration in the California ballot case.

Economy and markets.

Fed week. Wednesday's decision follows September's hike to three and three-quarters to four percent; markets lean to a hold, with December the live question. The dilemma: inflation of three point seven percent is energy-driven, and a policy rate cannot reopen Hormuz. Phillips-curve readers say hold, hike if expectations drift; supply-shock readers say hiking into an oil shock buys recession, not disinflation. Warsh gives no forward guidance, so the press conference is the signal.

Brazil's real fell more than eight percent Monday, its worst day since nineteen ninety-nine, and São Paulo stocks slumped. Markets had priced a Bolsonaro win. The fear is looser fiscal policy; the finance minister pick is the first tell. Overshoot, say some strategists; fiscal dominance, say others.

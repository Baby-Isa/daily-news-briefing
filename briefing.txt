Economy and markets.

United States. September inflation hit three point seven percent, up from three point four and above the three point six expected, driven by fuel; gasoline is up thirty-four percent on the year. Mechanism: an oil supply shock lifting headline prices after the Fed already hiked in September, so traders now weigh another hike at month's end. Intended effect: stop energy costs seeping into wages and expectations. Risk: monetarists say act now to anchor expectations; Keynesians say rates cannot produce oil and a hike deepens the slowdown, with the ten-year yield briefly above five point three percent. Read-through: higher-for-longer rates cut the present value of long-duration tech and raise the cost of debt-funded AI buildout. Chips led the fall, Tokyo shed five percent and Seoul halted trading. Anthropic told investors quarterly revenue rose fifty percent, slower than some hoped, ahead of its listing.

Economy. One. Today's US consumer price index print is the whole market. Wall Street came off record highs, oil and gold up, Korea's Kospi up over four percent on chip buying. Two. The yen, following on. Mechanism: after the 31 July joint US-Japan intervention, Treasury Secretary Bessent is now openly split with Prime Minister Takaichi over whether the Bank of Japan should hike, while Japanese government bond yields surge. Intended effect: intervention plus a September hike narrows the rate gap driving the carry trade and caps imported inflation. Risk: intervention without a policy move is the textbook failure case, because the portfolio-balance channel is weak and the signalling channel needs credibility that a public split destroys — one reading is the yen simply resumes sliding, the competing reading is that surging bond yields do the tightening anyway and pull Japanese capital home. Read-through: a real September hike unwinds carry positions, drains dollar liquidity and lands hardest on the long-duration AI capex trade, which is the dominant exposure here. Three.
